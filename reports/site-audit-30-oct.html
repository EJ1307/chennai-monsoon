Chennai monsoon site: audit

https://chennai-monsoon.vercel.app, checked 30 Oct 2026 against the one-page project plan (20 Oct)

How this audit was done, and its limits

  • Not independent. I built this site, so this is a self-audit. To offset that, I recomputed every core number in a fresh R script from the CSV the site publishes, without using the project's code.
  • Tested live: all six pages, both pickers on the first page (all 16 month and decade combinations), the Predict date box (in season, out of season, 29 Feb, empty, a year past the maximum), all five history buttons, the cut-off slider at off-grid values, the three fit buttons, both Decision sliders at their ends, the CSV download and an unknown page address. No script errors occurred.
  • Not available: only one of the two project PDFs (the one-page plan) and none of the screenshots were attached, so the second document could not be checked for contradictions. The R Shiny app was not re-tested in this round. The station's identity could not be confirmed with IMD directly.

1. Executive verdict

Yes, it is suitable for the project, after a short round of fixes. Every analysis in the plan is on the site, and every core statistic I recomputed matches to the decimal. I found no wrong formula and no critical error.

The problems are about communication and two modelling assumptions. The Predict page reads like a weather forecast. The 2.5 mm threshold and the "framework, not a warning system" caveat, both required by the plan, were lost when the text was shortened on 30 Oct. The heavy-rain chance assumes a wet day's size does not depend on the days before it, which the record does not fully support.

2. Project understanding

The plan asks one question in three parts. First, whether daily wet/dry occurrence at Nungambakkam in October to December (1991 to 2023, wet means 2.5 mm or more) behaves like a first-order Markov chain (H1 to H3). Second, whether that behaviour or the heavy-rain tail changed across three decades (H4, H5). Third, how to use the fitted model for a decision: a cost-loss flood-alert rule, with reservoir buffer days as the alternative if the chain reproduces dry spells.

The plan comes with:

  • an audit before any modelling, with no interpolation;
  • extreme-value fits (lognormal, gamma, Weibull) chosen by AIC and QQ plots;
  • return periods with bootstrap intervals over whole seasons.

The R Shiny app is named as a secondary deliverable that does not replace the report. This is a probability model of rainfall occurrence, not a storm forecasting system. The Predict page was added later, at Nilesh's request (29 Oct voice note), and is not in the plan.

5. Statistical audit

Correct and verified. Using the published CSV and a script written from scratch, I recovered the following exactly:

  • the transition counts and the first-order chain;
  • the H1, H2, H4 and H5 statistics and the ANOVA;
  • the 95th-percentile cut-off;
  • the truncated Weibull fit (shape 0.69, scale 18.8);
  • the 10-year return level;
  • the Decision page's heavy-rain chances (0.7% after two dry days, 3.4% after two wet days).

Pairs that touch a missing day are excluded, as the plan says. The H3 simulation uses the same season length as the record, so spells cut at 1 October and 31 December are treated the same way in both.

Weak points.

  1. Heavy-rain chance by state. The heavy-rain chance multiplies P(wet) by one fixed P(heavy | wet) = 5.25%. The record suggests heavy days cluster:
    • after two wet days, 4.8% of days were heavy, against the model's 3.4%;
    • after two dry days, 0.2%, against the model's 0.7%.
    This is the plan's own method, but the gap should be stated. It is visible on the Decision chart as the "record" ticks.
  2. Seasonality on the Decision page. The Decision page uses a season-average chain. The Predict page's AIC table shows seasonality is the largest single effect (ΔAIC 195 from flat to season only). So "heavy rain tomorrow after two wet days" is 3.4% on Decision, but 3.0% on 15 Oct and 3.8% on 1 Nov on Predict.
  3. "No trend" wording. "No trend since 1991 (p = 0.258)" and the decade results are absence of evidence, with only 8, 11 and 11 seasons per decade. They should read "no evidence of a trend".
  4. Buffer days. The reservoir buffer comes from the first-order chain that H3 rejects for dry spells. The seasonal model on Predict does reproduce the longest dry spells (97% of real seasons inside its 90% range), so it would be the defensible source.

6. Prediction model audit

  • What it predicts: the probability that a chosen day from 1 October to 31 December is wet (2.5 mm or more), and the probability it brings more than 101 mm.
  • Inputs: a date, and optionally the wet/dry state of the two days before.
  • The model: a logistic regression with three pairs of seasonal harmonics; for next day, also the last two days and a seasonal change in memory. It was chosen by AIC from five candidates, and the table is on the page.
  • Assumptions: no trend over years, so the year makes no difference; a wet day's size is independent of the date and of the days before; wet-day sizes come from one Weibull fit.

Is it meaningful? Yes, as a probability model, and it is tested honestly. Each season is hidden in turn and forecast from the others (2,640 days). The page shows how often it is right:

Where it overstates.

  • The page title "Will it rain on the day?" and the default date of 15 Oct 2027 invite reading the long-range number as a forecast. For any date more than two days away, it is climatology: the average chance on that date across 30 seasons, only 6% better than a flat guess.
  • "95% range 26% to 35%" is a confidence interval for that average chance, not a range for the day's weather.
  • The page does not say that it cannot see storms, cyclones or forecasts; a depression in the Bay of Bengal changes everything.
  • The heavy-rain number inherits the independence assumption above.

Rain and heavy rain are clearly separated, with two different numbers and labels.

8. Missing features

  • A plain limitations statement, required in spirit by the plan's caveats: rain gauge only, unlisted days read as dry, 30 seasons, no trend evidence, not a forecast.
  • The "framework, not a calibrated warning system" sentence (removed).
  • Season counts per decade.
  • Nothing else from the plan is missing. The analysis report and simulation code are separate deliverables and are not expected on the site.

9. Interface audit

  • Strong: light, clean layout. Charts carry axis labels with units, legends and log scales where needed. Every interactive control updates instantly. Phone layout has no horizontal overflow. Tables are consistent. Extrapolated return periods are greyed.
  • Weak:
    • The 30 Oct text cut went slightly too far: definitions, the threshold, the season count and one required caveat went with the padding.
    • Abbreviations such as "yest, yest2", "s, c" and "Brier skill" assume a statistics reader.
    • The first-page sidebar note disappears on phones.
    • The date box format differs from the rest of the site.
  • Nothing on the site is decorative or off topic.

11. Top five changes before the presentation

  1. Reframe Predict as a probability model, not a forecast, with a limitations line and correct interval wording.
  2. Put "Wet: 2.5 mm or more" back on every page, and restore "a framework, not a calibrated warning system".
  3. Add the two sentences linking H2 to the Predict model, and Decision's season average to Predict's dates.
  4. Show season counts per decade (8, 11, 11).
  5. Base the buffer recommendation on a model that passes H3, or on the record, and say so.

12. Final verdict

Suitable, but requires minor improvements. The statistics are complete and correct against the plan, and they reproduce independently. Every flag above can be fixed in text or with one R rerun, in an afternoon.

If it were presented as it stands, the likely challenges are flags 1, 3 and 6:

  • "Is this a forecast?"
  • "Where is your warning-system caveat?"
  • "Why recommend buffer days from a chain that failed H3?"
